Jarvis turned and bolted, lurching side to side, his gait thrown off by the rolling quakes.

Hoping luck was with him, Max mustered all the calm he could for another shot.

Jarvis cried out in surprise when the bullet hit. The little man struck the wall, spun, and then ran on, the shot likely a glancing blow absorbed or deflected by his plate carrier. He gained another few meters of lead ahead of Max and Anna.

On steady ground Max could have nailed him at forty meters in the narrow hallway, but the constant and violent shaking put Jarvis out of effective range, forcing pursuit. Jarvis led them through several disintegrating chambers and passages, his shorter legs surrendering ground to their longer strides. Stone morphed into black walls, smooth and metallic. Though the area shook, nothing fell from the lighted ceiling in this unaltered portion of the ship.

Twenty meters ahead, Jarvis reached a left-right intersection and glanced back.

Max fired on the run, his first shot ricocheting high above Jarvis’ head. The second hit center-mass—or would have if Max had fired a nanosecond earlier. Jarvis had already fled down the left passage. The slide locked back to the loading position, and Max dropped the empty pistol. The M9 held fifteen rounds in a standard magazine, but Goadish must have used a few to waste some guardians or perhaps his own men.

Don’t need it anyway! I’ll rip his throat out with my bare hands.

They passed from ship hallways back into stone, ran into a long room with more gold pillars. Max vaulted over three dead civilians pierced with dozens of darts, the victims of a trap. Jarvis’ lead had severely diminished by the time they entered a cave warren lit only by chem-lights every few meters. Corpses of mercs torn limb from limb lay scattered all over, yet the guardians had vanished. With the gateway self-destructing, Max figured their services were no longer necessary.

Rubble rained freely here, small stones constantly pummeling Max and Anna, yet they managed to avoid the larger chunks. Alas, so did Jarvis, who lost yet more ground while climbing and descending the slopes in the undulating tunnels.

The caves gave way to walls of worked stone. Jarvis had a mere fifteen meters on them, slowly dipping to ten as they ran through several hallways and rooms. Again Jarvis came to a left-right intersection, breaking right this time. Max and Anna reached the fork seconds later.

“Look!” Anna shouted, pointing left to where daylight shined at tunnel’s end about fifty meters away, obviously the main entrance. No sunshine to the right, only Jarvis’ frantic retreat, a string of flickering bulbs along the floor, lighting his way.

“Go! Get out! This is my fight!” Max pointed left as he ran right, not waiting for her to answer or comply.

She did neither, unfortunately, running a few steps behind him.

Where the fuck is Jarvis going? The roughhewn passage had narrowed to about eight feet, with many sharp turns Max had to carefully approach, for Jarvis might be waiting around any bend, ready to blow his head off. But the little bastard kept running, as the earthquake intensified, often knocking Max off balance to crash into a wall.

Jarvis hung a left. Max pursued heedlessly this time and nearly caught a bullet in the shoulder. Only a few meters ahead, Jarvis didn’t risk another shot and again took off. Daylight shined at the passage’s end about a hundred meters off.

“You’re mine, Jarvis!” Concentrating on the Accident Man, Max careened off one wall but kept sprinting. He closed to five meters, his stumbles in the shaking hallway be damned. Finally!

The earth trembled with its greatest intensity yet, and Max leapt on instinct. As he sailed through the air, the floor cracked into hundreds of fragments that dropped into a yawning pit of pure blackness. And then he ran on solid ground, lurched right and nearly crashed into a wall, got back on keel, and pursued.

Anna screamed from behind, stopping him dead.

Turning, he saw only her hands clawing at the pit’s edge, her fingers already losing purchase on the vibrating stone. Indecision tore at his conscience—avenge the dead or save the living? He watched Jarvis pull away, then glanced back to Anna.

“Dammit!” He growled as he ran back and knelt at the edge.

“Go!” Anna shouted, “Take your revenge!”

He ignored her words, grabbed her left wrist as her fingers slipped from the edge. He started hauling her up, almost dropping her when a large chunk of ceiling crashed down beside him and dropped into the pit, narrowly missing her. He hauled her over the edge, pulled her to her feet, and then glanced over his shoulder.

Jarvis’ silhouette slid up a wall for support as he scrambled to his feet after falling down. He had nearly escaped the tunnel.

“Come on! We can still catch him!” Max said.

The earth shook with another jarring tremor, followed by the boom of an explosion deep in the bowels of Zet. All three of them fell. Max landed atop Anna to shield her from falling rock and to keep her from rolling back into the pit.

“It’s the gateway!” she cried over the tumult.

“No shit!” Max stood the instant the rocks ceased falling, grabbed Anna, and ran.

Jarvis darted outside into the light, gone in a blink.

“Fuck!”

The rumbling changed pitch, reminding Max of the runaway train noise when Goadish had flooded the tunnels in Bolivia.

“Go! Don’t look back!” Anna shouted.

But like Lot’s wife, Max couldn’t help himself.

He wished he’d heeded her words. Blinding light filled the tunnel. A wall of writhing, white-hot flames shot toward them. Anna took the lead when he slowed, and Max sprinted at her heels as daylight beckoned. The rumbling grew deeper by the moment. Even as the hungry flames sucked fresh air for oxygen, a surge of heat burned his exposed skin.

Five meters from the exit, Max pushed back his roasting agony and shouted, “Faster!”

He burst from the tunnel, got a glimpse of his surroundings, and shoved Anna toward a ditch filled with muddy water. She yelped. Tripping she splashed down on her back.

Max made his leap as the flames roared from the tunnel. The searing thrust propelled him into the water. His overheated skin chilled with a shocking suddenness. Anna struggling beneath him, but he pushed her to the bottom as the blast roiled the water’s surface inches above his back, immediately reviving the sear of his burns.

One breath of that superheated air might be enough to kill them. While Anna thrashed for freedom and oxygen, Max kept her pinned down. Though his own lungs screamed for air, he fought his instinct to surface until he could take it no longer.

They broke the surface and gasped for air, and then gagged for it when they breathed in the acrid dust kicked up by the explosion. Unable to escape it, they coughed for perhaps a couple of minutes. The ash and grit slowly settled, revealing the brushfires that surrounded them.

At the familiar mechanical thump of a helicopter rotor in the turbid air, Max stood. “No!” He bolted toward the sound.

“Max!” Anna called, but he didn’t wait for her.

Down a muddy trail, he stumbled through smoke and haze. At a single gunshot, he increased his pace.

A black helicopter loomed through the dispersing dust, its rotor kicking up eddies amongst the outlines of several trailers.

Faster! Move your ass! Max burst through foliage at the edge of the helipad as the bird’s engine accelerated with a supercharged whine, the rotor raising a sandstorm as the chopper took off.

Max leaped for the skids of the helicopter. His outstretched fingers just brushing against the metal, unable to gain a purchase, as he dropped to the deck. He glared up as it banked mere feet above his head, his vision momentarily clear despite the hurricane blast of drying wind from the rotor. Burt Jarvis, the murdering dwarf he’d sought for so many years, stared down at Max with deadpan blue eyes, one corner of his mouth raised in sneering triumph.

And then he was gone.

Near Max’s feet lay a dead merc, blood still leaking from a hole in the back of his head. He snatched the merc’s rifle and opened up on the fleeing chopper in full auto mode as dust again washed over him. He fired through the blinding haze; the thirty-round mag exhausted within a few seconds.

No smoke from the chopper, no hiccups from its wailing engine. It flew on through the murky air and disappeared.

Unleashing a primal scream that hurt his own ears, Max collapsed to his knees in abject frustration. Never had he felt such immense rage, such a sense of helpless futility. In a fleeting instant, a flood of memories flashed through his mind: David and Janet’s coffins at their funeral; Banner screaming in the red-stained snow; Juno standing before him with her katana; the prehistoric monster towering above him in the Guyana jungle; Swift dying in the burning warehouse; Goadish sliced open and bleeding out—every one of the thousand hells he had endured; every drop on the bloody trail that had brought him so close to fulfilling his obsession.

Twice Max’d had him, and twice Jarvis’d escaped.

Fuck! I didn’t even know it was him! He thought again of Janet and David, their images symbolizing and cementing his failure.

His next enraged bellow could be heard for kilometers around.
